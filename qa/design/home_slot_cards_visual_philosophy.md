# Home Slot Cards

The home slot cards are miniature cabinets, not banners. Each card should sell the physical promise of a slot machine before the player enters the game: glass depth, lit side rails, reel wells, a calm marquee zone for the image title, and a grounded control dock for the play button. The composition must look meticulously crafted, with every bevel, lamp, reflection, and shadow placed as if the asset was built by hand for a premium casino interface.

Violet Fortune is jewel-forward. Its card uses violet glass, cyan rim light, magenta cabinet glow, faceted corner accents, and bright symbol gems arranged like a small preview of the real reels. The center must feel luminous without becoming noisy; the title and play image overlays need dark, stable backing so the Russian labels remain readable.

Roman Reels is architectural and ceremonial. Its card uses aged gold, marble darkness, column-like side rails, bronze reel separators, and a restrained cyan spark. It should feel heavy and valuable, but still mobile-readable at 360dp. Ornament is concentrated in the frame and corners while the reel window stays crisp.

Both cards must be image-first and store-ready: no embedded CTA text, no real-money implication, no cashout cues, no external casino visual language beyond harmless social-casino entertainment. The result should feel like master-level app art: dense enough to reward inspection, disciplined enough to remain clear when scaled down.
