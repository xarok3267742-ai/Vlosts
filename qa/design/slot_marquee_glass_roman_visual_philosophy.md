# Roman Marquee

The Roman cabinet should feel like polished arcade hardware built from warm marble, bronze, and ceremonial gold. It must share the same engineered silhouette as the Violet machine while changing the material language enough that the player immediately reads a different slot identity. Every bevel is placed with painstaking attention and the final layer must look calibrated against the cabinet by a master-level visual craftsman.

Light is warmer and heavier here. Amber LEDs, pale stone reflections, and thin turquoise guard lines create a premium social-casino object without implying real-world gambling value. The visual information stays in form and material; there is no explanatory text, only transparent structure that supports the reels.

The top bridge acts like a glass-covered temple lintel: dense at the crown, restrained near the symbols, and clean in the center. The composition must remain image-only, decorative, and readable on narrow devices. Craft means the asset appears labored over, with no accidental clutter over the first reel row.

The design should make Roman Reels feel like its own machine, not a reskin. Gold nodes, marble veins, and bronze rails must be precise enough to look expensive, yet subtle enough that symbols, bets, and spin controls stay dominant.
